## South Australia: Form A1 (December 2025)

Compulsory from 1 January 2026. Each prospective tenant over 18 completes a separate form. A person who deviates from the form must not mislead (Legislation Interpretation Act 2021 s52). Prospective tenants may attach more documents voluntarily.

Completed by the landlord or agent: 1 address; 2 exclusions; 3 duration (periodic, 12 months or other fixed term) and date available; 4 rent and frequency (fixed amount, no invited higher offers, no more than 2 weeks rent in advance); 5 bond (up to 4 weeks, or 6 weeks if rent is over $800 a week); 6 rates and charges (water, electricity, gas: tenant, landlord or not applicable; other charges; whether the property is advertised or under a sales agency agreement for sale); 8 landlord or agent details including Registered Land Agent number; 9 documents requested; 11 tenancy databases (up to 3, names and contacts, unless given within 7 days before); 12 ways to submit (up to 3).

Completed by the applicant: 3 preferred duration and move-in date; 7 full name, current address, email, phone 1 and 2, joint application with, and whether all prospective tenants are over 18 with legal capacity; 9 acknowledgement of requested documents and any other identity document proposed; 10 two referees (name, relationship, contact details) and other relevant referee information; 13 signature and date.

Documents: no more than 2 identity documents (driver's licence, passport, birth certificate, proof of age card; at least one with a photo), and no more than 2 for ability to pay (bank statement with outgoing transactions and account numbers redacted, payslip or document confirming employment, proof of pension or other income, other document on financial capacity).

Rules on the form: nothing about employment beyond a payslip or a document confirming employment (regulation 8(1)(h)); applicants cannot be required to say whether they will seek bond assistance; no payment other than rent or bond (s53); data must be protected and destroyed 30 days after the successful tenant signs the agreement, or, with the applicant's consent, as soon as practicable 6 months after the application date; no fee for giving a person their listed database information; false information carries a $20,000 maximum penalty.

Consequence for ranking: the form has no income figure, no employer, no rental history and no pet question. SA ranking can use only the referees, the move-in date and lease preference, and which documents were provided. We say so on screen.

Sources: official Form A1 (Consumer and Business Services, December 2025); sa.gov.au "Choosing a tenant" summary; Reapit and Onya summaries for dates (secondary).

## Western Australia: Form 18

Official form from the Department of Local Government, Industry Regulation and Safety (Consumer Protection), Residential Tenancies Act 1987 (WA), file dated July 2025. Up to three applicants on one form; we collect one person per submission and link them.

Applicant fields: name, date of birth, phones (home, work, mobile), email, current address, proof of identification cited (for example driver's licence, passport, birth certificate); previous rental history (address, rent paid and frequency, rent paid to, their phone and address, rented from and to, reasons for leaving); employment (occupation, employer, employer address, employed from and to, contact name, phone, net income and frequency, net weekly income from other sources); two personal references (name, relationship, phone, address); next of kin (name, relationship, phones, address); emergency contact (same).

Agent-completed: agent contact details; Attachment A written notice of tenancy databases used (name, operator contact details, how to obtain information); list of up to four, and the applicant must be told within 7 days if they are listed.

Notices on the form: application does not form part of a tenancy agreement; option fee (capped at $50 or $100 depending on rent, returned within 7 days if unsuccessful); equal opportunity (no discrimination on sex, marital status, pregnancy, gender history, parental or carer status, sexual orientation, race, religious or political conviction, disability, mental health, age or association). The Consumer Protection tenant guide also says rent bidding is not allowed.

Open questions: whether use of Form 18 is compulsory; whether the form's references, next of kin and emergency contact are all required of every applicant; retention rules (the form states none). The form has no signature or consent field. We add a privacy acknowledgement and typed name, labelled as ours and not part of the official form.

An option fee is a payment between applicant and agent. Open Home Hero does not take it, and the notice says so.

## Northern Territory: approved information (version 2, 2 February 2026)

Commissioner of Tenancies, s18A Residential Tenancies Act 1999. Guidance: ask only what helps decide who to rent to. Not relevant and possibly discriminatory: marital status, age, gender, sexuality, intention to start a family, race, nationality, health or medical history, religious or political beliefs, disability, domestic violence, arrests, convictions. Bank statements are private information. Age may be asked only as "meets eligibility criteria" or "of legal age". Destruction duties are in s18A(4) and keeping rules in s18B (not read in full).

Approved information: name, email and telephone; legal age to enter a tenancy agreement; meets housing criteria (if a requirement); current address; details of previous or current landlord and consent to contact them for a reference; referee contact details and consent to contact them (a referee may be a landlord, employer or other person who can vouch for identity, ability to pay or care of property); number of occupants including the number of children under 18; current income; whether they have pets; emergency contact; other relevant information.

Approved documents: identity, one primary document (driver's licence, proof of age card, Defence Force ID, government ID, passport, Working with Children or Ochre card, photo ID from a recognised local Aboriginal authority) and up to two secondary documents (land council letter, Medicare card, concession or health care card, birth, marriage or divorce certificate, Centrelink income assessment, income tax notice of assessment, utility bill, immigration or citizenship papers, Department of Housing proof of identification form). Ability to pay, up to two primary documents (current payslips, income statement from employer or Centrelink, letter on letterhead from employer, income tax notice of assessment if self-employed, a document showing investment income and frequency); bank statements only if no primary documents are available and the applicant must be able to redact them; consent to contact the employer, with employment status information (job title, self-employment, net salary, net pay or gross annual salary). Ability to care for the property: reference from a previous landlord, neighbour or personal associate. A "document" means a type of document (two payslips count as one).

Source: official PDF, signed by the Commissioner of Tenancies, 2 February 2026 (read by screenshot).

## New South Wales

No prescribed form or list was found. The Fair Trading Commissioner's guidance (tenancy applications) says: collect only what is reasonably necessary; ask only prospective tenants who will be on the lease; sight identity documents and do not store copies; capacity to pay may be shown with a current rental ledger or payslips, or redacted bank statements showing savings if there is no employment income; likelihood of looking after the property may be shown by a previous landlord or agent reference or confirmation that the tenant has read the Tenant Information Statement; a tenancy database may be searched if the applicant is told; do not ask for credit reports; do not charge for background checks (only holding fee, rent, bond and registration fee may be charged, s23 Residential Tenancies Act 2010); remove marital or relationship status questions; delete unsolicited information; explain why each item is needed. Rent bidding: landlords, agents and digital rental application providers must not solicit higher offers.

Pets: from 19 May 2025 NSW landlords may refuse a pet only on prescribed grounds through the tribunal, so our NSW form does not ask about pets and the ranking does not use pets.

Open questions: whether any prescribed items exist in the Residential Tenancies Regulation 2019 that I could not read; retention periods; whether Open Home Hero counts as a "digital rental application provider" under the rent bidding rule.
